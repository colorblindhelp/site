See the web through different eyes.

ColorBlind Helper is a free Chrome extension that shows how any webpage looks with protanopia, deuteranopia, tritanopia or monochromacy, and helps you spot low-contrast elements before they reach your users.

Add to Chrome, it’s free (opens in a new tab)

Listed on the Chrome Web Store as “Color Blind Mode.” Built for designers, developers, accessibility testers, and anyone curious how color reads to others.

1 in 12men, and about 1 in 200 women, have some form of color vision deficiency.

Color is often the only thing carrying the message.

Red for errors, green for success, a chart legend made of colored dots. With typical color vision it’s easy to miss when those cues stop working. ColorBlind Helper lets you check on the real page, in a couple of clicks.

Source: UTMB Digital Accessibility (opens in a new tab)

Test real pages, not screenshots.

ColorBlind Helper applies its filter to the website in your current tab, so you can scroll and review the page the way you normally would.

Eight modes, grouped the way vision differs

Choose from milder anomalous forms through to complete deficiencies, plus a High Contrast mode. Pick one from the dropdown and press Apply filter.

  • Red-green: deuteranomaly, protanomaly, deuteranopia, protanopia
  • Blue-yellow: tritanomaly, tritanopia
  • Other: achromatopsia (monochromacy) and High Contrast

Simulations use color transformation matrices based on published research by Brettel, Viénot and others.

Find the text people can’t read

Light grey copy, pale placeholders, buttons that fade into the background: low contrast is one of the most common accessibility problems on the web. Switch to High Contrast mode to make low-contrast elements stand out, then fix them at the source.

Contrast matters to everyone, not only people with a color vision deficiency. It decides whether text holds up on a phone in sunlight or a dim projector.

    Stays out of your way

    No accounts, no setup screens. It lives in your toolbar until you need it.

    One click
    Open the popup, choose a mode and press Apply filter.
    Any website
    Works on the pages you open in Chrome, including your own.
    Remembers your mode
    Your last choice is saved, so the filter carries over to new pages.
    Runs locally
    Everything happens in your browser. Nothing is sent to a server.

    From install to first preview

    Four steps, and the last one is just switching it off.

    1. Add it to Chrome

      It’s free. Open the Chrome Web Store listing and choose Add to Chrome

      Open the Chrome Web Store (opens in a new tab)

    2. Pin it to your toolbar

      Click the puzzle-piece Extensions icon and pin Color Blind Mode so it’s always one click away.

    3. Pick a mode and apply

      Open the popup on any page, choose a simulation from the dropdown and press Apply filter

    4. Switch back to Off

      Set the dropdown to Off whenever you want to see the page normally again.

    “Being a person who knows the struggles of having colorblindness, I’m passionate about building tools that help people with the same struggles that I face.”

    Hi, I’m Mihir Sanghvi, a computer science student at Rutgers University. I’m in love with the idea of building tools that help people.
